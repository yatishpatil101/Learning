# Decision ledger

Every question raised while the platform was built, and its answer. One line each.
This file records **decisions**, not narrative — the story of each build is `tasks/todo.md`,
and the coverage claim is `e2e/COVERAGE.md`.

Rules for this file: a decision gets a row, never a section. When a row's work ships,
the row stays (it is the record of *why*) and nothing else is written anywhere else.
Do not restate a decision in `todo.md` — link to the number.

| 45 | Should the Enquiries desk's status writes move to the server? | **No — and this is the one place in the admin wave where "move it to the backend" is correctly answered no rather than "not yet".** The obvious reading of the console was that it had four write controls (Responded, Close, visit Completed/Cancel/Reschedule) with no endpoint behind them, and therefore four endpoints waiting to be written. It is the opposite. `contact_requests.status` is not an ops workflow field: `ContactRequestStatuses` documents `pending` as *awaiting the owner's decision* and `canTransition` permits only `pending → approved \| declined`, both of which are the **owner's** answer about their own contact details. A desk that wrote "responded" into that column would not be recording ops progress, it would be forging a consent decision the owner never made, and the V4 CHECK would be the only thing standing between the console and a database that says an owner agreed to something they were never asked. So there is no missing route to name here; there is a column that deliberately belongs to somebody else. The four controls were **deleted** rather than ported (they wrote to `localStorage` via `mutateDb` and were `!live`-gated, so on a live build they rendered nothing and on a mock build they told the operator "Visit cancelled" while no visitor was ever told anything). "Responded" survives because it had a real destination all along — `addNote` against the listing, which has a provider on both sides — and is now unconditional, so both modes perform the same action instead of two different ones. **The visible defect this uncovered was on the read side, not the write side:** the "Open leads" tile counted `new \|\| open`, which are the browser store's words; the server emits `pending \| approved \| declined` and never those two, so the tile rendered a confident `0` on every live build while three requests sat unanswered on the board below it — measured, then reproduced as a failing assertion (`Expected: "3", Received: "0"`). | 264 | Are a pre-leased commercial listing's in-place rent and lease expiry public, or behind the contact gate? | **Public, on the card and the detail page, with no gate.** The commercial branch now collects `tenancyStatus`, and when it is `leased` it also collects `inPlaceRent` and `leaseExpiry`. The instinct to gate them is the one that gates a mobile number, and it is wrong here for a reason specific to this asset class: **a pre-leased property is bought for its yield, and yield is a ratio of these two numbers to the asking price.** A buyer who cannot compute it cannot decide whether the listing is worth an enquiry at all, so gating the figure does not delay the decision, it prevents the shortlist — and shortlisting is the step the search page exists to serve. The contact gate protects a *person's* reachability; these are *terms of the asset*, the same kind of fact as the carpet area or the floor, and an investor comparing four godowns needs all four side by side. **What makes it a decision rather than an obvious call:** the in-place rent is a commercial term the sitting tenant negotiated and may reasonably consider confidential, and publishing it tells that tenant's competitors what they pay. The countervailing fact is that the seller is both the party with the confidentiality interest *and* the party typing it into an optional field — the wizard does not require `tenancyStatus` to be answered at all, and a seller who does not want the number public leaves it blank, which is a different statement from `vacant` and is stored as such. The control already sits with the person whose interest is at stake, so a gate would take a choice away rather than give one. **Not revisited unless** the field stops being optional, or a lease term arrives that the seller cannot decline to answer. |
