Once you have seen a property and agreed the broad terms, the deal panel on the [property page](/listings) takes it the rest of the way — an offer, a negotiation, and a close that both sides have to agree to.

## Making an offer

Sign in, open the property, and use **Make an offer**. You give an amount and, for a rental, when you would move in.

You have **one offer per property**. Changing your mind does not create a second offer — it updates the one you already made, and the previous amount stays visible in the history so neither side can rewrite what was said.

## The back and forth

| State | Whose turn |
| --- | --- |
| Pending | The owner's — you have made an offer and are waiting |
| Countered | Yours — the owner has come back with a different number |
| Countered by you | The owner's |
| Accepted | Agreed |
| Declined | Finished, for that offer |

You can accept the owner's counter outright, or counter it back. There is no limit on the rounds.

> [!NOTE]
> An accepted offer is an agreement on price. It does **not** close the property — that is a separate step below, which is what lets you agree a number and still walk away after the paperwork check.

## Under Offer

An owner can mark a property **Under Offer** while they work through a shortlist. It stays visible and searchable; it is a signal, not a removal, and the owner can add or remove the interested parties behind it.

## Closing

Closing is deliberately two-sided:

1. You use **Request to finalize**.
2. The owner accepts.

On the owner's acceptance three things happen at once: the property is marked sold or rented and comes off the market, **every other pending finalize request on that property is automatically declined**, and — for a rental — your tenancy record is created so rent, documents and your [Rent Passport](/help/a/paying-rent-online) have something to attach to.

You can withdraw your own request any time before the owner acts on it. If the owner declines, you can ask again; declining has no other effect.

> [!WARNING]
> Only one buyer wins. If you have requested finalization on several properties, the first owner to accept closes you out of nothing — but every *other* buyer on that property is closed out immediately. Do not treat a pending request as a placeholder.

## For owners

From your own listing you close directly; you do not have to wait for a request. You will see:

- An offers inbox, with declined offers hidden
- A finalize inbox with a **waiting on you** count
- **Mark under offer**, **Close deal** and **Reopen**

A finalize request from someone with a verified tenant profile is badged as such — see [Tenant profiles](/help/a/tenant-profiles).

Reopening a closed property puts it back on the market. It does not undo a tenancy that was created.
