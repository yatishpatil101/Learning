If you are buying a resale flat in Pune, the seller's agent, your bank and your lawyer will all ask for one document early on: the **Index II** (often written "Index 2"). It is short, it is usually in Marathi, and it tells you a great deal about the property's last registered deal.

## What Index II is

When a property document is registered at a Sub-Registrar's office in Maharashtra, the office records it in indexes kept under the Registration Act, 1908. **Index I** is arranged by the names of the people involved. **Index II** is arranged by the property, and records the details of that one transaction.

So an Index II is a one-page summary of a single registered document, such as a sale deed, an agreement for sale, a gift deed or a mortgage.

## What it shows

An Index II normally lists:

- **The type of document,** for example an agreement for sale.
- **The price paid** (consideration) and the **market value** as per the government's ready reckoner rate.
- **The property:** village, survey or CTS number and, for a flat, the society, wing, flat number and floor.
- **The area** of the flat or land.
- **The parties:** the seller (executant) and the buyer (claimant), usually with addresses and PAN.
- **The dates** on which the document was signed and registered.
- **The stamp duty and registration fee** paid.
- **The document number,** made up of a serial number, the Sub-Registrar office code and the year.

> [!TIP]
> Match the names, flat number and area on the Index II with the sale agreement the seller shows you. Any mismatch is a question to put to your lawyer before you pay a token amount.

## How to download it online

The Inspector General of Registration (IGR) Maharashtra runs a free search, linked from [igrmaharashtra.gov.in](https://igrmaharashtra.gov.in). You do not need to log in.

1. Open the free search and choose the right tab. Pune city and its suburbs usually fall under the urban areas outside Mumbai.
2. Select the **year** of registration, then the **district**, **taluka** and **village**.
3. Enter the **survey, CTS or flat number,** fill in the captcha and search.
4. If you know the **document number,** you can search by registration type, Sub-Registrar office, year and serial number instead.

Each search covers one year, so you need to know roughly when the flat was last sold. Online records for most of Maharashtra go back to around 2002.

> [!NOTE]
> The free copy is for information. Some banks and courts ask for a certified copy from the Sub-Registrar office that registered the document, which carries a small copying fee. Check with your bank which one it needs.

## Index II, 7/12 extract and property card

These three are often confused:

- **Index II** comes from the registration department and records a transaction. It shows who sold to whom, not necessarily who owns the property today.
- **The 7/12 extract** (Satbara utara) is a revenue record for land, mostly agricultural or rural plots.
- **The property card** is the City Survey record for land in urban areas.

For a flat in a housing society, you will mostly deal with the Index II of each previous sale, along with the society's share certificate.

## Why buyers and banks ask for it

- **Title check:** a lawyer uses the Index II of each earlier sale to trace the chain of ownership.
- **Home loan:** lenders ask for it for resale flats. See our [home loan documents checklist](/blog/home-loan-documents-pune).
- **Resale and inheritance:** it is proof that a sale or transfer was registered.

Remember what it does **not** show: unregistered agreements, society transfers, pending court cases and loans that were never registered. A full title search by a lawyer covers these.

## Before you buy

Get the Index II for every past sale of the flat, and have a lawyer read them alongside the other papers.
