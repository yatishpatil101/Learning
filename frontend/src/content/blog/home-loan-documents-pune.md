A home loan moves only as fast as your paperwork. Most delays happen because a single document is missing or does not match another. Gather the list below before you apply, and the bank's checks go much faster.

Every lender has its own list, so treat this as a starting point and confirm with your bank.

## Your identity and address

- **PAN card.** This is compulsory.
- **Identity proof,** such as Aadhaar, passport or voter ID.
- **Address proof,** such as Aadhaar, passport, or a recent utility bill.
- **Passport-size photographs** and a filled application form.

If you are applying with a co-borrower, such as your spouse or a parent, they will need the same set.

## Your income

**If you are salaried:**

- Salary slips for the last three months.
- Form 16 or income tax returns for the last two years.
- Bank statements for the last three to six months, showing your salary credits.

**If you are self-employed:**

- Income tax returns for the last two to three years, with the computation of income.
- A balance sheet and profit and loss statement, audited or certified by a chartered accountant.
- Proof of business, such as GST registration or a Shops and Establishment licence.
- Business and personal bank statements for the last six to twelve months.

## Property documents: new flat from a builder

- **The agreement for sale** or allotment letter.
- **The MahaRERA registration number** of the project. Learn [how to check MahaRERA registration](/blog/check-maharera-registration).
- **Approvals** such as the commencement certificate and approved plans. Banks often have these on file for projects they have already approved.
- **A builder NOC** in favour of the bank and **receipts** for what you have paid so far.
- **A tripartite agreement** between you, the builder and the bank, which many lenders use for under-construction flats.

## Property documents: resale flat

- **The registered sale deed or agreement** of the current owner, and earlier ones that show the chain of ownership.
- **[Index II](/blog/index-2-pune-property)** for each registered sale.
- **A society NOC** and the **share certificate**.
- **The occupancy certificate** (OC) or completion certificate.
- **Recent property tax and maintenance receipts.**

Your bank's lawyer will run a title search on these papers, and a valuer will inspect the flat before the loan is sanctioned.

## How much the bank can lend

The Reserve Bank of India caps the loan-to-value (LTV) ratio, that is, the share of the property's value a bank can lend:

- **Up to ₹30 lakh:** up to 90%.
- **₹30 lakh to ₹75 lakh:** up to 80%.
- **Above ₹75 lakh:** up to 75%.

The rest is your down payment. Stamp duty and registration are usually paid from your own pocket on top of it, so plan for those too. Our [stamp duty guide](/blog/stamp-duty-flat-pune) shows the numbers for a typical Pune flat, and the [EMI calculator](/emi-calculator) shows what the loan will cost each month.

## Tax benefits in 2026

The Income-tax Act, 2025 has applied since 1 April 2026. Under it:

- **The old tax regime** still allows a deduction of up to ₹2 lakh a year for interest on a loan for a self-occupied home, and up to ₹1.5 lakh for principal repayment within the overall investment limit.
- **The new tax regime** does not allow these two deductions for a self-occupied home. Interest on a let-out property is still deductible.

Which regime suits you depends on your other deductions, so ask a tax adviser before you decide.

> [!TIP]
> Check that your name is spelt the same way on your PAN, Aadhaar, salary slips and the sale agreement. Small mismatches are one of the most common reasons for a delay.
