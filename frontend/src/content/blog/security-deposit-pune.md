The security deposit is usually the largest single payment in a rental, and the most common reason owners and tenants fall out. Most of those disputes can be avoided with a few lines in the agreement and a few photos on move-in day.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is general information, not legal advice. For a dispute, speak to a local advocate.

## How much do owners in Pune ask for?

There is no fixed rule. For flats in Pune, a deposit of **two to three months' rent** is common, and some owners in premium societies ask for more. Furnished flats often carry a higher deposit than unfurnished ones because there is more to repair or replace.

Everything is negotiable. Tenants with a steady job, good references and a longer lock-in are often able to bring the deposit down.

## Is there a legal limit?

Not at present. The Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 does not cap the deposit, so the amount is whatever the owner and tenant agree.

You may have read that deposits are now capped at two months' rent. That limit comes from the central government's **Model Tenancy Act, 2021**, which is only a template for states. Maharashtra has not adopted it so far, so the two-month cap does not apply in Pune. Be wary of social media posts that say otherwise.

> [!TIP]
> The refundable deposit also affects the stamp duty on your rent agreement. Stamp duty counts 10% a year of the deposit as notional interest, so a larger deposit means slightly higher stamp duty. See our guide to [rent agreements in Pune](/blog/rent-agreement-pune).

## Put these points in the agreement

Most deposit disputes happen because nothing was written down. Your leave and licence agreement should state:

- **The amount** and how it was paid, with the transaction reference.
- **The refund timeline,** for example within 7 or 15 days of handing back the keys.
- **What can be deducted:** unpaid rent, unpaid bills and damage beyond normal wear and tear.
- **That normal wear and tear is not deducted,** for example faded paint, minor scuffs and the natural ageing of fittings.
- **The notice period,** and what happens to the deposit if either side leaves early.

Pay the deposit by bank transfer, not in cash, so there is a clear record.

## Record the flat's condition on day one

On move-in day, walk through the flat with the owner and make an inventory of the furniture, appliances and fittings. Take dated photos or a video of every room, including existing marks, cracks and stains. Both of you should sign the inventory and keep a copy.

When you move out, repeat the walk-through against the same list. It turns an argument into a simple comparison.

## What owners can fairly deduct

**Usually fair:**

- Unpaid rent, including for the notice period
- Pending electricity, water or gas bills
- Broken fittings or appliances, and items missing from the inventory
- Other damage caused by the tenant

**Usually not fair:**

- Repainting after a normal stay
- Faded curtains, worn mattresses and normal wear on taps and switches
- Deep cleaning when the flat was left clean
- Repairs to things that were already broken at move-in

If the owner wants to deduct something, ask for the bill or a written estimate.

## If the owner does not return the deposit

1. **Ask in writing.** Send a polite message or email with the agreement clause, the move-out date and your bank details. Keep it factual.
2. **Send a legal notice.** If there is no reply, a notice from an advocate is often enough to settle the matter.
3. **File a recovery suit.** As a last resort, you can file a case in the competent civil or Small Causes court in Pune. A local advocate will advise you on the right forum.

A **registered** agreement helps you here. In Maharashtra, if the owner did not register the agreement, the tenant's version of the terms is presumed to be true unless the owner proves otherwise.
